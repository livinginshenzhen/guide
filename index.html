Preparing to Move

This is what and how much you can ship.

Air Cargo Shipping

Usually the allotment is 100 cubic feet (2.83 m³) per person – roughly 20 large moving boxes, though packaging material eats into that fast. The shipment usually doesn't leave the US until your residence permit is granted, so expect delay even after you've landed. It also isn't airtight, so sachets of activated charcoal in each box (especially ones with fabric) helps with the musty smell that sets in after weeks in a warehouse. We've seen it take 5 weeks from residency permit to delivery fairly consistently.

What Cannot Ship

  • Valuables – cash, jewelry of any value, precious stones, high-value collectibles, silverware
  • Pressure-sensitive goods – food, beverages or alcohol, toiletries, cosmetics, aerosols/perfumes, cartridges (including printers with cartridges installed)
  • Books – capped at 50 regardless of family size, due to weight/density
  • Specific items – cameras, drones, weapons of any kind, refrigerators containing Freon, medicine, toxic/flammable/radioactive materials, religious content/items, any newly-purchased packaged items
  • Most magnets – if it would deflect a compass placed next to it, it's too magnetic to ship
  • Any batteries – items can ship with batteries removed, but the batteries themselves have to travel with you in cabin luggage

Visualize the 100 cubic feet allotment against everyday items at upakweship.com before you start packing – it's easy to overestimate how much that actually is.

Money & Banking

Getting money into China takes planning – wires, ATMs, and WeChat all have quirks that are easier to avoid than fix after the fact. Adding credit cards to use in WeChat is simple, but US banks often block charges from China. WeChat can often block charges from hitting the banks.

Moving Money via Wire Transfer

Funds can be wired in USD or RMB – the currency you choose changes the process significantly.

Wiring USD

  • Requires an in-person bank visit to exchange it into RMB
  • ~$50,000 annual deposit limit
  • Expect a fair amount of paperwork and signatures during the exchange

Wiring RMB

  • No exchange step required
  • No annual deposit limit
  • Wiring bank will ask for paperwork on the money's origin/purpose

Account Holder Name Format Matters

Recall exactly how your name is formatted on your bank paperwork – "LastFirst" or with a space, and where the capital letters fall. WeChat requires an exact match to link your card or bank account.

Other Methods to Move Money

Don't forget to notify your bank.

Call customer service proactively to whitelist international payments. If you have authorized users on the same card, simultaneous payments may also trigger a block.

Wise App (Recommended)

Transfer funds from your US bank to a Chinese bank (via UnionPay ATM card) using Wise. You can transfer up to RMB¥30,000 (about USD$4,400) per transfer with about a USD$100 transfer fee. Initial setup requires passport-image and face-scan verification, but subsequent transfers are quicker.

ATM Transfers

Usually you can only withdraw RMB¥3,500 per trip (about USD$500), and there may be a fee on top of that.

Transfers via WeChat from US Banks

WeChat's own transfer feature claims to accept direct transfers from a US account, but it hasn't worked well in practice – stick to Wise or ICBC for now.

Passport & Consulate Guide

There is no US Consulate in Shenzhen. The nearest is in Guangzhou, reachable by high-speed train. Any minor, or any person with a 5-year validity passport, must go to the consulate in person. Both parents should plan to attend, or bring sufficient consent documents if only one parent can come.

Book Your Appointment

Use the ACS scheduling portal for the Guangzhou Consulate. Aim for a morning slot – afternoons are more crowded.

Consulate Prohibits Electronic Devices

No phones, smartwatches, or any electronics are allowed inside the consulate building. Staying within walking distance lets you leave devices in your hotel room. The consulate does offer free phone storage inside (they hand you a cubby token) – but smartwatches must stay behind entirely.

What To Do With Your Expiring Passport

Option A – Keep It & Mail Later

Take your expired passport home with you after the appointment. When your new passport is ready (4–8 weeks), mail the expired one to the consulate via EMS.

Best if you have travel planned – you can keep using your old passport in the meantime.

Option B – Leave It There

Leave the expired passport at the consulate with your application. Simpler, but you won't have it for travel until the new one arrives.

Best if you have no travel planned before the new passport arrives.

Note: High-speed train travel in China requires your passport for booking and boarding. If you leave your expired passport at the consulate, you won't have any passport on hand – which may make this option unworkable if any train travel is anticipated before the new one arrives.

Either way: Both passports return to you via EMS, requiring payment on delivery – around ¥22. You'll need to be home, or your building's front desk can receive and pay on your behalf.

After You Receive Your New Passport

Two more required steps once it arrives:

1. Update TRRF at Your Police Station

All foreigners must update their Temporary Residence Registration Form at their local police station after any passport change.

2. Renew Your Visa at the Civic Center

Visa expiration dates are tied to the passport they were issued in. You must renew your visa after getting a new passport. It's recommended to book an appointment.

Internet & VPN

China actively defends against foreign internet traffic. All traffic leaving or entering China is inspected – foreign services are blocked, VPNs are unreliable, and speeds to foreign servers are slow. Local apps like WeChat operate on infrastructure that bypasses this entirely.

Questions To Answer Before You Move

A whole-house VPN router makes sense if you're home often and need consistent US access. If you mostly need occasional secure browsing, a VPN app on your phone may be enough – with the caveats below.

Do you need live US content (sports, streaming) or are downloads acceptable?

How much of what you want to access is US-licensed only?

How often will someone be at home needing foreign internet access?

How concerned are you about device security and traffic monitoring?

Recommendation

Get your own VPN service before you arrive. Once inside China, it can be very difficult to download and set up a VPN. An international eSIM is your most reliable fallback when VPNs fail.

Mobile Phones

Nearly every transaction, payment, and app in China requires verification via a local phone number. Getting the right phone setup is one of the first things to sort out.

Getting a Local Phone Number

Recommendation

Visit a China Unicom or China Mobile store in person with your passport. Significant identity verification is required. Some expats keep a separate China-only phone to avoid spam apps and improve security.

Physical SIM Card

The standard for years. Works with any phone that has a SIM slot. Apply at any China Unicom or China Mobile store – bring your passport.

eSIM (New as of Early 2026)

Some foreigners are now allowed to get a local eSIM, but may require a mainland Chinese iPhone or a select Android model compliant with GSMA standards.

International eSIM as a VPN Bypass

Bypasses the Great Firewall Entirely

Cellular data from a foreign carrier eSIM routes through non-Chinese infrastructure, bypassing the GFW completely. It's not fast or cheap enough to be your main connection, but it's a reliable fallback when VPNs fail – including during ISP outages.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1

Before Departure: Fill Out China's Electronic Entry Card

Before you leave, complete China's electronic entry card online and save the QR code it generates. You'll use it during immigration on arrival in Shekou – even though your passport will usually pull up your info automatically, it's good to have as a backup.

2

Deplane → Follow Signs to "Immigration"

Upon exiting the aircraft, follow overhead signs towards Immigration / 入境檢查. Look for signs also showing Mainland/Macao (內地/澳門) – this is the direction you want.

You will see blue directional signs overhead. The key indicators are: Immigration, Transfer Desks E1, E2, and Mainland/Macao.

3

Board the Internal Airport Train

You must take a short automated train to reach the immigration and ferry terminal area. Follow the train icons on the signage.

Once off the train, continue following signs for Mainland/Macao (內地/澳門). You'll look for Transfer Desk E2 on the left.

4

Locate the Ferry Ticket Counter

Look for the CKS SkyPier Ferry Ticket Counter. Do not clear Hong Kong immigration. The counter handles ferries to both Macau and Shenzhen (Shekou).

Go to the counter designated for Shekou / 蛇口 (Shenzhen). Do not join the Macau queue by mistake.

5

Purchase Your Ticket to "Shekou" & Inform the Agent

Ask for the next available ferry to Shekou (蛇口). At the counter, you will hand over your checked baggage tags (if you did not have the bags originally checked through to ZYK). The ferry service will collect and load your bags directly onto the ferry. You will NOT collect your bags in Hong Kong. You pick up your checked luggage only after arriving at Shekou.

The SkyPier ferry accepts credit cards – you do not need to exchange cash before boarding.

6

Take Train to Airside Ferry Terminal

At the appointed time, you'll show your ticket and then take the internal airport train to the airside ferry terminal. Note that there is a 7-Eleven outlet near the ferry gates.

7

Board the Ferry & Cross to Shekou

The ferry journey takes approximately 45–60 minutes. The ferry is comfortable with indoor seating. Your carry-on luggage can be stowed near the entry doors.

8

Collect Your Baggage

Upon arrival at Shekou Ferry Terminal (蛇口客运港), proceed through immigration. Then, downstairs via an escalator, collect your checked bags from the baggage belt at the terminal.

9

Head to the Ride Hailing Area

After Customs, take the elevator to the 西 West Ride Hailing Area and order a Didi.

Important Reminders

Arrive at the Ferry Counter at Least 45 Minutes Before Departure

The baggage handover, ticket processing, and boarding procedure take time. Missing the ferry cutoff is a real risk if you linger. Move briskly from your gate to the SkyPier area.

Through-Checked Baggage Is a Major Convenience

The SkyPier service allows your checked luggage to be transferred directly from your incoming flight to the ferry – you never handle your bags at HKIA. Just hand over your baggage tags at the ticket counter and collect at Shekou. Confirm this service is active when you purchase your ticket.

Backup Plan – If You Miss the Ferry or Accidentally Clear HK Immigration
Critical: Once You Clear Hong Kong Immigration, You Cannot Return to the SkyPier Ferry

The ferry terminal is airside. Clearing HK immigration means you have exited the restricted zone – there is no re-entry to take the ferry. You must use an alternative route.

Alternative Route via Shenzhen Bay Checkpoint

CTG Bus (中旅巴士) – Mainland/Macao Transport Area

1
Clear Hong Kong immigration (if not already done) and collect your checked baggage from the baggage reclaim carousel.
2
Clear Hong Kong Customs and proceed to the Arrivals Hall.
3
Follow signs to "Mainland/Macao Transport" (往內地/澳門交通) – located in the ground transportation area, signposted from the Arrivals Hall.
4
Find the CTG Bus (中旅巴士) ticket counter. Purchase a ticket specifying your destination: Shenzhen, Shekou, crossing via the Shenzhen Bay (深圳灣) checkpoint. The agent will direct you to the correct bus bay (Bay 32–34 area for limos/coaches).
5
The bus/limo will cross the Shenzhen Bay Bridge border crossing. Expect the total journey to take approximately 1.5 – 2.5 hours depending on border wait times – significantly longer than the ferry.

Electrical Outlets & Plugs

The apartments have too few outlets. Here are the plug types you'll find:

Type A / Type I

One two-flat-pin outlet and one three-pin outlet. Any two-pin US plugs (most consumer electronics and chargers) will work in the Type A slot. Most of the outlets are this style.

Type A / I + USB-A

Same as previous with built-in USB-A ports for charging without an adapter.

Universal

Accepts Type A, Type C, and Type I, but there aren't many of these.

Voltage Mismatch

The US uses 110V / 60Hz and China uses 220V / 50Hz. The difference is significant enough to destroy non-compatible devices (e.g., small appliances), but most modern consumer electronics will be dual-voltage. If you bring a US-only appliance and plug it into an outlet, it will be damaged or destroyed.

Safe to Plug In (Dual-Voltage)

Most modern electronics auto-detect and handle both voltages. Look for "100-240V" on the label:

  • Laptops
  • Phone & tablet chargers
  • Camera battery chargers
  • Most modern power adapters

Do NOT Plug In (Single-Voltage)

These items underperform or be a fire hazard. Check the label.

  • Cheap hair dryers
  • Some hair straighteners and curling irons
  • Older kitchen appliances
  • Anything with a fixed-speed motor
