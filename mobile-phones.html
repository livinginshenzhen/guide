Mobile Phones

Nearly every transaction, payment, and app in China requires verification via a local phone number. Getting the right phone setup is one of the first things to sort out.

Getting a Local Phone Number

Recommendation

Visit a China Unicom or China Mobile store in person with your passport. Significant identity verification is required. Some expats keep a separate China-only phone to avoid spam apps and improve security.

Physical SIM Card

The standard for years. Works with any phone that has a SIM slot. Apply at any China Unicom or China Mobile store – bring your passport.

eSIM (New as of Early 2026)

Some foreigners are now allowed to get a local eSIM, but may require a mainland Chinese iPhone or a select Android model compliant with GSMA standards.

International eSIM as a VPN Bypass

Bypasses the Great Firewall Entirely

Cellular data from a foreign carrier eSIM routes through non-Chinese infrastructure, bypassing the GFW completely. It's not fast or cheap enough to be your main connection, but it's a reliable fallback when VPNs fail – including during ISP outages.

Recommended eSIM Providers

Trip.com

  1. Download from a non-China app store before you arrive.
  2. Purchase a "Mainland China 5G eSIM" plan.

Plans vary by size & duration; appear to auto-renew.

You may also use your VPN to allow ordering eSIMs.

BNESIM

Offers non-expiring eSIM plans – data can persist for as long as you have the eSIM, and you can add other countries to the same eSIM. Good choice if your usage is irregular.

Download from a non-China app store before you arrive.

You may also use your VPN to allow ordering eSIMs.

Both providers route traffic directly through their countries so you'll be able to access Google, Facebook, and the like, but some US sites (e.g., regional banks, utility companies) may still block these connections.

iPhone Recommendations

Recommendation

Use an iPhone 13 or older (physical SIM), or a foreign iPhone from Canada, Japan, UK, or South Korea (physical SIM + eSIM).

iPhone 14+ (US model) – eSIM only. Won't work directly with Chinese carriers.
iPhone 13 and older – 1 physical SIM + eSIM. Works great in China.
China-model iPhones – 2 physical SIM slots, but Apple Intelligence is unavailable even when you travel internationally.
Foreign iPhones (CA, JP, KR, UK) – Physical SIM + eSIM = ideal travel combo. Find them secondhand in China or on eBay.

Android Recommendations

Recommendation

Any Android with a physical SIM slot should work. Samsung phones auto-switch their store locale to Chinese when a Chinese SIM is installed – handy for finding local apps.

Samsung US models may get delisted by Chinese carriers. Most Samsungs have 1 physical SIM + 1 eSIM, so you can survive on eSIM while you sort it out.
Chinese Android phones – many are US-banned. If you buy one here, it likely won't work back home. Xiaomi is a rare exception (barely).

Hong Kong & Macau Roaming

HK and Macau don't support mainland Chinese carriers. Roaming is usually a nominal RMB¥30/day – budget for it before you hop on that ferry.
