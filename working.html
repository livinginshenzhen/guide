Working Days

Although you’ll have unlimited paid time off, be aware of the days the office will be closed and teams will not be working. You may also consider not traveling domestically during these periods – or risk traveling with extreme crowds. The company will release its official calendar only after the government releases mandatory minimum days off directive at the end of each year.

An important note: Some weekend days are assigned as working days in order to cluster more days off around holidays – especially Chinese New Year and Mid-Autumn, the two major week-long breaks. The longer holiday block is often referred to as a “golden week” – for its potential in tourism and commerce.

Working Hours

Shenzhen, specifically, seems to take pride in its “996” philosophy – that is working 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., 6 days a week. While the sixth work day isn’t usually observed, don’t assume it will always be considered a day off.

Typical Work Hours (US)

You’ll find most employees arrive at 9 a.m. and leave at 6 or 7 p.m. Lunch usually is taken at 11:30 a.m., followed by a nap or walk until 1:30 p.m. Note that scheduling meetings during this period should be avoided. Generally, the higher the level, the less this pattern is followed.
